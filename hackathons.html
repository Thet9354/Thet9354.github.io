Lion City Hacks
Lion City Hacks — Most Entertaining Project 🏆
Lion City Hacks · Red Hat Singapore · 3 December
Team SmashBros — a gamified remedy for wrist pain, powered by AI. Took a dry medical-adjacent problem (repetitive strain from long sessions at the keyboard) and wrapped it in a game loop so the "treatment" was something people actually wanted to do. The Most Entertaining Project award was voted by the attendees themselves, not the judging panel — exactly the audience the build was designed for.
"This was my first Lion City Hacks and honestly the first time I felt old in a hackathon — most of the room were literal kids spending the whole day coding, full of vigour and enthusiasm. So we deliberately chose to design something that would resonate with that crowd: a gamified, fun take on a wrist-pain remedy, instead of the usual serious enterprise pitch. Winning Most Entertaining Project from the attendees told us we'd hit the brief — broadening horizons and grabbing the attention of younger developers, not just impressing judges."

Create4Good
SUTD x ESSEC Create4Good Hackathon
SUTD Venture, Innovation & Entrepreneurship x ESSEC Business School · 10–12 March 2023
Brought HearAll back as the team's submission — by now a more refined Android build with a tighter recognition pipeline and a real validation plan attached. Surveyed eight hard-of-hearing users on the prototype: 87.5% found it useful, 75% found it accessible to people unfamiliar with technology. Presented the build, the methodology, and the numbers to a full auditorium on the final day.
"Doubling down on HearAll across hackathons was the decision. Each event became another iteration loop — T4G 2022 gave the product its first contact with real users, iNTUition proved we could harden it under pressure, so Create4Good was for closing the loop: prove people actually wanted it, with numbers. Running the same product through back-to-back briefs is a real founder muscle — the idea, judges, and time budget all change, but the thesis has to keep holding."

iNTUition v9.0
iNTUition v9.0
IEEE NTU Student Branch · 25–26 February 2023
Rebuilt HearAll under 24-hour pressure. After T4G 2022 surfaced what real users actually needed, iNTUition was the sprint to harden the build: a tighter recognition loop, a cleaner live camera flow, and a sharper pitch — all reshipped between Friday night and Saturday afternoon, against the clock.
"Bringing HearAll back into a 24-hour sprint was a deliberate constraint. Instead of starting fresh, the brief became 'take what the assistive-tech community told you at T4G and ship the next version by tomorrow afternoon.' 24 hours is too short to design a brand from scratch; it's the right length to prove a single iteration of an existing one."

Tech for Good 2022
Tech for Good (T4G) 2022
Engineering Good x Singapore Institute of Technology · Supported by S&P Global Foundation & James Dyson Foundation · 2022
Where HearAll started. T4G 2022 was the origin point for HearAll — an Android accessibility app using AI sign-language recognition to translate signs into text in real time. Built the first Android MVP for this national assistive-tech showcase themed "Bringing Assistive Technology Closer to the Community," then iterated through to an iOS port. Walked the milestones on the main stage and ran a booth where the assistive-tech community could try the recognition flow firsthand.
"T4G was the genesis. Picking an assistive-tech showcase as HearAll's first outing wasn't accidental — building accessibility tech for a room full of judges teaches you to demo, but building it for the actual community teaches you whether the product is real. Their feedback — what they tolerated, what they discarded, what survived contact with daily use — set the roadmap for every hackathon iteration after this one."

ITI 2023
ITI 2023 — Integrating Persons with Disabilities into the Workplace
Nanyang Technological University Welfare Services Club (NTUWSC) · 2023
Pitched an inclusion-oriented extension of the HearAll thinking — taking the same accessibility thesis from earlier hackathons and pushing it into the workplace, where the harder problems are systemic (meetings, hiring, onboarding) rather than purely technical. The submission was an ideation concept rather than a build, defended in a room of disability advocates, industry leaders, and persons with disabilities themselves.
"ITI was where I deliberately stepped back from 'build a feature' and into 'design a workplace.' Choosing this event was a decision to widen the HearAll mission: real inclusion needs more than an app, it needs the surrounding processes. Defending an idea in front of people who live the problem is the cheapest reality check a builder can get — and it reframed what kind of product HearAll should grow into."

MXHacks 2022
Mendix × Siemens · 2022
Built an admin dashboard for logistics companies on Siemens' Mendix low-code platform — a working ops console designed for a real enterprise use case, shipped without a traditional codebase and pitched to the Mendix × Siemens organisers.
"MXHacks pushed me out of my usual stack and onto a low-code platform. Picking a logistics admin dashboard as the build was a deliberate choice — the domain has obvious enterprise value and the data model is messy enough that low-code's strengths (forms, tables, workflows) actually shine. Shipping something real on a tool you've just picked up, against the clock, is a fast lesson in focusing on the problem instead of the tooling."
